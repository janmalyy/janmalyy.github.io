### Large Language Models in Medicine

In my introductory [article]({{ "/blog/2026/05/28/me-and-computational-linguistics.html" | relative_url }}) on computational linguistics, I&nbsp;mentioned 
that I&nbsp;had chosen an interdisciplinary topic for my master's thesis, linking large language models with bioinformatics and genetics. 
I will describe what exactly lies behind this and what I&nbsp;have been working on for the&nbsp;past few months.

In my thesis, I&nbsp;focused on the&nbsp;automatic interpretation of genetic variant mentions in biomedical literature using large language models (LLMs). 
I developed an open-source web application that helps molecular geneticists streamline their daily workflow when assessing the&nbsp;pathogenicity of genetic mutations in patients.

### From Finding a&nbsp;Problem to a&nbsp;Functional Web Application

Just like with my volunteering activities in Hnutí Brontosaurus (more [here]({{ "/blog/2026/05/28/2026-05-30-volunteering-in-hnuti-brontosaurus.md)" | relative_url }}), 
where it is essential for me to see a&nbsp;tangible result behind my work, I&nbsp;wanted to create something practical for my thesis as well.

I chose the&nbsp;field of medicine and bioinformatics because it makes sense to me to use technology where it can truly help someone.

I reached the&nbsp;final topic gradually, and I&nbsp;must admit I&nbsp;was quite surprised by how specialized it turned out to be, but I&nbsp;took it as a&nbsp;challenge.

During consultations with experts from CEITEC, we defined how I&nbsp;could help them. 
Molecular geneticists receive tables of mutations found in patients, and for the&nbsp;lesser-known ones, they must manually go through scientific articles to determine whether the&nbsp;mutations are dangerous. 
This process is tedious and demanding because there are simply too many papers. the&nbsp;resulting web application accelerates and streamlines article browsing.

### What It Taught Me

The master's thesis was a&nbsp;test for me in several areas. It showed me that:

- **I can drive a&nbsp;project to completion independently**: From the&nbsp;initial user requirements analysis through architecture design, 
dealing with missing API documentation, all the&nbsp;way to deploying a&nbsp;functional prototype as a&nbsp;web application.

- **I acquired new technical skills**: I&nbsp;learned to validate model outputs using pydantic-ai, deployed the&nbsp;application via Docker to Koyeb, and practiced prompt engineering alongside XML and JSON parsing.

- **I can orient myself in an unfamiliar field**: I&nbsp;did not know what HGVS nomenclature or ACMG/AMP guidelines were, or what the&nbsp;difference was between PubMed and MEDLINE – but I&nbsp;looked it up and managed to put it to use.

I look forward to leveraging the&nbsp;experience gained in my future professional practice!
